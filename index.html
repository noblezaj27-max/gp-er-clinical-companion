Quick safety checklist

  • Confirm patient identity, weight, age, allergies, pregnancy status where relevant.
  • Verify units, concentration, route, frequency, maximum dose and renal/hepatic adjustments.
  • For high-alert infusions, use an independent double-check and smart-pump/library policy.
  • Reassess clinical response and document the source/protocol used.
  • Escalate early if unstable or if required diagnostics, monitoring or definitive treatment are unavailable.

Medication dose & volume calculator

Enter the dose from a current, verified drug reference. The calculator does not contain a drug database and cannot determine the appropriate medication or dose.

Continuous infusion calculator

For an already-authorized prescription only. This tool does not recommend a starting dose or titration target. Confirm drug, diluent, compatibility, concentration, line, pump library, dose range and monitoring. Independently double-check all vasoactive and other high-alert infusions.

Electrolyte & chemistry calculators

Simplified sodium mass-balance estimate

This is a simplified estimate, not a correction prescription. It does not account adequately for ongoing urine/electrolyte losses, changing renal function, or dynamic physiology. Sodium correction requires frequent laboratory monitoring and a patient-specific plan to avoid overcorrection. Follow current institutional protocol and specialist guidance.

Corrected calcium estimate

Formula estimate: measured Ca + 0.8 × (4 − albumin). May be inaccurate in critical illness, CKD and acid-base disorders; use ionized calcium when indicated.

Potassium infusion planning reminder

Do not use a generic calculator to determine potassium replacement. Before administration, verify potassium concentration, route, maximum peripheral/central concentration, infusion rate, renal function, urine output, ECG/monitoring requirements and repeat-lab interval against your hospital protocol. Never administer concentrated potassium by IV push.

ABG interpretation worksheet

Pattern aid only. Assess clinical context, sample type, compensation, mixed disorders, lactate, electrolytes, oxygen delivery and ventilatory support. Do not base treatment on this interpretation alone.

Clinical quick guides

Unstable patient / shock checklist

  1. Call for help; assign team roles and activate local emergency/transfer pathway.
  2. Assess airway, breathing, circulation, disability and exposure; monitor SpO₂, ECG, BP, temperature and mental status.
  3. Establish appropriate access; obtain focused history, exam, bedside glucose and indicated investigations without delaying stabilization.
  4. Identify likely shock category and reversible cause; reassess after each intervention.
  5. Document trends, response, escalation and transfer communication.

This is a generic safety checklist, not a resuscitation algorithm or fluid/vasopressor prescription.

ECG systematic review

  1. Confirm patient, date/time, calibration (usually 25 mm/s and 10 mm/mV) and lead placement.
  2. Rate and rhythm; P waves and relationship to QRS.
  3. Axis; PR, QRS and QT/QTc intervals.
  4. QRS morphology, bundle branch patterns, voltage and hypertrophy.
  5. ST segments, T waves, pathologic Q waves and comparison with prior ECG.
  6. Correlate with symptoms, vitals and serial ECG/troponin when indicated; escalate suspected time-critical ischemia or dangerous arrhythmia.

Dengue / febrile patient safety checklist

  • Assess warning signs, hemodynamic status, perfusion, urine output, oral intake and bleeding.
  • Trend hematocrit and platelet count in context; a single platelet count does not define severity or fluid requirement.
  • Use current DOH dengue guidance and phase-specific monitoring; avoid automatic fluid rates.
  • Escalate shock, respiratory distress, significant bleeding, altered sensorium, poor perfusion or inability to monitor safely.

No dengue fluid prescription is generated in this app.

OB emergency readiness checklist

  • Call obstetric, anesthesia, neonatal and blood bank support as appropriate.
  • Assess maternal ABCs, vitals, bleeding, uterine tone, gestational age and fetal status where relevant.
  • Establish access, obtain indicated labs/crossmatch and prepare escalation/transfer.
  • Use current hospital obstetric emergency protocols for hemorrhage, severe hypertension/eclampsia, sepsis, shoulder dystocia and cord prolapse.
  • Document timing, events, medications, response and handover.

No medication doses or procedural instructions are supplied; follow current specialty protocols.

Neonatal / pediatric emergency readiness

  • Use an accurate current weight; for neonates, confirm gestational and postnatal age.
  • Use age-appropriate equipment and locally approved pediatric/neonatal resuscitation algorithms.
  • Check concentration and dose in mg/kg AND total mg AND mL before administration.
  • Confirm maximum dose, interval, renal adjustment and monitoring.
  • Escalate early when pediatric airway, monitoring, blood products or definitive care are unavailable.

ACLS / PALS

Use the current official resuscitation algorithms and certified training materials. This app intentionally does not reproduce proprietary algorithm charts or provide drug-dose tables.

Additional bedside calculators

Mean arterial pressure (MAP)

Approximation: (SBP + 2 × DBP) / 3. Less accurate with marked tachycardia, arrhythmia or abnormal waveforms; use direct measurement when available.

Shock index

Shock index = heart rate / systolic BP. Interpretation varies by age and clinical condition; not a standalone diagnostic test.

Anion gap

Anion gap = Na − (Cl + HCO₃). Optional albumin adjustment = AG + 2.5 × (4 − albumin). Reference intervals are laboratory-dependent.

Adult BMI

Cockcroft–Gault estimated creatinine clearance

For adults with reasonably stable creatinine only. Selecting an appropriate body weight requires clinical judgment. Not valid for neonates, children or rapidly changing kidney function.
